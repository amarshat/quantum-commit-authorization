# Empirical fee-cost result

Source: `fees/data/basefee.csv.gz`, Ethereum mainnet, 2024-07 to 2026-07, 40 contiguous 4096-block runs. Base fees fetched
from a public archive RPC by fees/fetch.py, committed, and replayed
offline here; gas numbers come from bench/results. Regenerated by
fees/analyze.py and drift-checked in CI.

## Break-even multiples

The flow beats a direct-verification transaction unless the base fee
rises by more than m* between commit and reveal, where
m* = (verify_total_gas - commit_gas) / reveal_gas:

| flow | baseline | m* |
|---|---|---|
| action | ETHFALCON (Keccak PRNG, non-FIPS) | 15.2x |
| action | ML-DSA-44 (NIST) | 79.3x |
| auth_only | ETHFALCON (Keccak PRNG, non-FIPS) | 22.7x |
| auth_only | ML-DSA-44 (NIST) | 118.6x |

Rows marked * are candidate minCommitAge values. Even at the p99.9
tail the ratio stays far below the break-even multiples above, which
is the empirical core of the cost argument.

## How often the flow was actually more expensive

Fraction of historical entry points where the two-transaction flow
cost LESS than one direct-verification transaction:

| gap g | flow | vs ETHFALCON | vs ML-DSA-44 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | action | 100.000% | 100.000% |
| 2 | action | 100.000% | 100.000% |
| 4 | action | 100.000% | 100.000% |
| 8 | action | 100.000% | 100.000% |
| 16 | action | 100.000% | 100.000% |
| 32 | action | 100.000% | 100.000% |
| 64 | action | 99.996% | 100.000% |
| 128 | action | 99.888% | 100.000% |
| 256 | action | 99.712% | 99.999% |

A user reveals as soon as the commitment ages, so the operative gap
is minCommitAge (4 to 32 blocks), where the flow was cheaper than
both baselines at 100% of historical entry points: no base-fee move
in two years came close to the break-even multiple over that few-
block window. The economics only flip in the long tail, and only
when an adversary censors the reveal toward commitTTL (256 blocks):
even at a full 256-block delay the flow still won 99.71% of
the time against the cheapest Falcon verifier, and was never beaten
by the standards-compliant ML-DSA-44 verifier at any gap. This is
the honest, data-backed version of the benchmark's single-number
break-even, and it is the fee-spike model the mempool simulator
draws from: a base-fee spike large enough to flip the cost is also
rare enough to measure, and it requires censorship the game already
prices separately.
